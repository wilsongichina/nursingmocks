## What Topics Are Covered on ATI TEAS Science?

ATI TEAS Science is organized around four content areas: Human Anatomy and Physiology, Biology, Chemistry, and Scientific Reasoning. NursingMocks keeps those areas connected to practice, not long textbook lessons.

### Human Anatomy and Physiology Practice Questions

Human Anatomy and Physiology practice focuses on body systems, structure and function, and how major processes work together. Expect questions that may involve systems such as cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous, digestive, endocrine, immune, urinary, reproductive, integumentary, or musculoskeletal systems.

This is the largest scored Science area, but do not treat it as memorizing organ names only. Many missed A&P questions happen because the student knows the parts but misses the relationship, such as how a structure supports a function or how one system affects another.

### Biology Practice Questions

Biology practice focuses on cellular processes, biological organization, heredity, genetics, and the use of genetic information. These questions may ask you to connect a process to its purpose rather than simply recall a definition.

Watch for wording that asks what happens before, after, increases, decreases, or changes. Small qualifiers matter in Biology, especially when the question is testing a sequence or a cause-and-effect relationship.

### Chemistry Practice Questions

Chemistry practice focuses on properties and changes of matter, chemical bonds, reactions, solutions, acids and bases, and basic chemical relationships. You do not need to turn this page into a full chemistry course to practise well.

When reviewing Chemistry misses, separate the problem into two parts: the concept and the wording. Sometimes the science is familiar, but the answer is missed because the question asks for the opposite, the product, the reactant, or the best comparison.

### Scientific Reasoning Practice Questions

Scientific Reasoning practice focuses on evidence, experiments, observations, variables, charts, tables, and conclusions supported by data. These questions can look easier than anatomy or chemistry, but they often punish rushed reading.

Do not choose an answer just because it sounds scientifically reasonable. The better answer is the one supported by the evidence, setup, graph, table, or observation in the prompt.

## ATI TEAS Science Question Formats and Problem Styles

ATI TEAS questions can appear in multiple-choice and alternate item formats across the exam. NursingMocks Science sets may vary by set, so the most useful thing is to read the prompt carefully and answer using the format shown.

The bigger difference is usually the problem style. Science questions may test concept application, structure-and-function relationships, scenario interpretation, diagrams, graphs, tables, experiments, or evidence-based conclusions. A graph question and an anatomy question can both be Science, but they require different habits.

## How Science Practice Works on NursingMocks

Open a Science set, read the prompt carefully, and answer the question based on the information provided. After the set, check the correct answers and review explanations when they are included.

Do not stop at the score. A score tells you what happened; the review tells you why it happened. If you missed several questions from the same content area or kept misreading the same type of prompt, that pattern is more useful than one isolated mistake.

## Timed ATI TEAS Science Practice

The official Science section gives 60 minutes for 50 questions, which averages about 1.2 minutes per question. That average is helpful, but it is not a rule for every item.

Some direct knowledge questions should move quickly. Scenario questions, data interpretation, and experiment-based prompts may take longer. The main pacing mistake is spending too much time trying to rescue one hard question while easier points are still waiting.

## Review Your Science Answers, Explanations, and Results

When you review a Science set, group missed questions by the reason they were missed. Missing a question usually comes down to one of four patterns:

- **Knowledge gap:** You did not know the fact, term, process, or concept.
- **Relationship error:** You knew the parts but misunderstood how they connect.
- **Prompt interpretation error:** You missed a qualifier, scenario detail, or requested outcome.
- **Evidence error:** You misread a chart, table, experiment, observation, or data pattern.

This kind of review keeps Science practice from becoming random. If most misses are knowledge gaps, go back to the topic. If most are evidence errors, practise questions with graphs, tables, and experimental setups. If the problem is prompt interpretation, slow down on words like not, least, except, increase, decrease, before, and after.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What topics are on ATI TEAS Science?

ATI TEAS Science includes Human Anatomy and Physiology, Biology, Chemistry, and Scientific Reasoning. NursingMocks organizes Science practice around those areas so you can work through questions without mixing in the other ATI TEAS subjects.

### How many questions are in the ATI TEAS Science section?

The official ATI TEAS Science section has 50 total questions. Forty-four are scored, and six are unscored pretest questions.

### How much time is allowed for ATI TEAS Science?

The official Science section allows 60 minutes. That averages about 1.2 minutes per question, but some short knowledge questions may take less time while data or scenario questions may take longer.

### Which ATI TEAS Science area has the most questions?

Human Anatomy and Physiology has the largest scored allocation, with 18 scored questions. Biology has 9, Chemistry has 8, and Scientific Reasoning has 9.

### Are NursingMocks Science questions official ATI questions?

No. NursingMocks is an independent practice resource and does not provide official ATI questions. The questions are for practice and review, not a copy of the official exam.

### How should I use my ATI TEAS Science practice results?

Use the results to choose your next Science activity. Look for the mistake pattern behind the score: knowledge gaps, relationship errors, prompt interpretation errors, or evidence errors.

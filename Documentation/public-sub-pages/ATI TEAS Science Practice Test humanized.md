## What Topics Are Covered on ATI TEAS Science?

ATI TEAS Science is organized around Human Anatomy and Physiology, Biology, Chemistry, and Scientific Reasoning. Those areas overlap at times, but they do not ask for the same study habit.

For practice, separate the work. A body-system question may reward accurate recall. A Scientific Reasoning question may reward evidence checking. A Chemistry question may turn on one word in the prompt.

### Human Anatomy and Physiology Practice Questions

Human Anatomy and Physiology questions focus on how the body is organized and how major systems work. Practice may involve systems such as cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous, digestive, endocrine, immune, urinary, reproductive, integumentary, or musculoskeletal systems.

The common trap is treating A&P as a list of parts. Many questions care about function and connection: what a structure does, how a process changes, or how one system affects another.

### Biology Practice Questions

Biology questions focus on living systems, cellular processes, biological organization, heredity, genetics, and the use of genetic information. These questions often ask you to connect a process with its purpose.

Read direction words carefully. Before, after, increases, decreases, produces, and results in can change the answer. If the relationship is backward, the answer can sound familiar and still be wrong.

### Chemistry Practice Questions

Chemistry practice covers properties and changes of matter, bonds, reactions, solutions, acids and bases, and basic chemical relationships. The goal is not to turn this page into a chemistry course. The goal is to practise the decisions that show up in Science questions.

When reviewing Chemistry misses, ask whether you missed the concept or the wording. Product, reactant, opposite, best comparison, acid, base, solution, and concentration are small words with real consequences.

### Scientific Reasoning Practice Questions

Scientific Reasoning questions test how you use evidence. You may need to identify variables, interpret observations, read a graph or table, compare data, or choose a conclusion supported by an experiment.

Do not pick an answer only because it sounds scientific. Go back to the prompt. The right answer has to fit the setup, chart, table, observation, or data given in the question.

## ATI TEAS Science Question Formats and Problem Styles

ATI TEAS questions can use standard and alternate item formats across the exam. On NursingMocks, answer using the format shown in the Science set rather than assuming every set uses the same question type.

The bigger difference is the problem style. One Science question may test direct recall. Another may ask you to connect structure and function, interpret a scenario, read a diagram, compare data, or judge whether a conclusion follows from an experiment.

## Timed ATI TEAS Science Practice

The official Science section allows 60 minutes for 50 questions, which averages about 1.2 minutes per question. That average is useful, but Science does not feel evenly timed.

Some questions are quick if you know the fact. Others slow you down because they include a scenario, chart, experiment, or unfamiliar wording. Do not burn too much time trying to rescue one hard question while easier points are still available.

## Review Your Science Answers, Explanations, and Results

Do not put every missed Science question in the same bucket. Most misses come from one of four places:

- **Knowledge gap:** You did not know the fact, term, process, or concept.
- **Relationship error:** You knew the parts but misunderstood how they connect.
- **Prompt interpretation error:** You missed a qualifier, scenario detail, or requested outcome.
- **Evidence error:** You misread a chart, table, experiment, observation, or data pattern.

This keeps review practical. If most misses are knowledge gaps, go back to the topic. If the misses are evidence errors, practise more chart, table, and experiment questions. If prompt wording keeps catching you, slow down on words like not, least, except, increase, decrease, before, and after.
